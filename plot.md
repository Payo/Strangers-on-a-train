# Plot / Story Arcs

## Fidelity
Stay very close to the true sequence of events. The story needs little or no fictional embellishments or romanticising. Light shaping of emphasis and pacing only.

## Essential Turning Points
1. James enters the compartment, smiles, then gets off the train (flight response to being overwhelmed by Norah’s beauty).
2. He returns at the next stop and they talk for the rest of the journey.
3. At Bristol, Norah looks out of the window, sees the station, grabs her suitcase and jumps out, barely saying goodbye.
4. James places a newspaper advert in the Sunderland paper describing her.
5. Norah’s mother sees the advert and telephones her (while Norah is still on holiday).
6. Norah invites James; they meet over tea and toast and fall in love.

## Central Dramatic Question
Will James find Norah again after she jumps off the train without giving him her name?  
A “snatching victory from the jaws of defeat” narrative.

## Modern Frame
Brief opening (a descendant beginning to tell the family origin story) and brief closing (the same descendant noting the lifelong result and the gentle contrast with modern dating). The bulk of the ~12 minutes stays inside 1924.

## Vonnegut Fortune Shape (James)
Wobbly at the start, then a series of progressive steps toward happiness and love:
- Good: sits opposite Norah
- Ill: leaves the carriage
- Good: returns
- Ill: no name or address
- Good: places the advert
- Good: mother sees it
- Good: tea and toast
- Good: lifelong romance

## Notes
No major invented events. The age difference, war background, and post-war context are lightly felt rather than dramatised.
