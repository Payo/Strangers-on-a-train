# Voice-Over Script — Norah and James

How I Met Your Mother style. Continuous narration by a descendant.  
Approximate total: 10–12 minutes. Timings are guides; stretch or compress in the edit to fit the picture.

---

**Opening (over black or soft fade-in)**  
This is the story of how my great-grandparents met.  
It begins on a bright morning in Sunderland, in the spring of 1924.

**Scene 1 — Leaving Sunderland (Shots 1.1–1.2)**  
Norah left the house with her suitcase and a book.  
She was on her way to stay with a cousin near Bridgwater.  
Just an ordinary journey.  
Or so she thought.

**Scene 2 — Changing Trains at Newcastle (Shots 2.1–2.2)**  
At Newcastle she changed for the Bristol train.  
The platform was full of steam and noise.  
She found a compartment, put her case on the rack, and settled in with her book.

**Scene 3 — James Boards and Leaves (Shots 3.1–3.2)**  
Just before the train pulled out, a young man stepped in.  
Fair hair, round glasses.  
He smiled at her, put his cases up, then suddenly patted his jacket as if he’d forgotten something, and stepped back out.  
The train began to move.  
Norah watched him go, a little puzzled.

**Scene 4 — The Long Conversation (Shots 4.1–4.2)**  
At Durham the train stopped.  
Norah looked out at the cathedral.  
And then he was back.  
A bit embarrassed, carrying a newspaper.  
He sat opposite.  
They started talking.  
Hours passed.  
Stations came and went.  
The book stayed closed.  
By the time the light changed, they were easy with each other.

**Scene 5 — Bristol: Norah Jumps Out (Shots 5.1–5.2)**  
At Bristol, Norah looked up, saw the sign, and gathered her things.  
She said good-bye — simply, the way you do when you expect never to see someone again — and stepped off.  
The train pulled away.  
And only then did James realise he didn’t know her name.  
Or where she lived.  
Or how to find her.

**Scene 6 — James at the Red Lion (Shot 6.1)**  
That evening he told a friend the whole story over a drink at the Red Lion.  
By the end of it he had made up his mind.

**Scene 7 — Placing the Advert (Shot 7.1)**  
The next morning he went to the post office and dictated an advert.  
Anyone who knew the young woman with blue eyes and a bobbed haircut who had travelled from Sunderland to Bridgwater on the fourth of April should write to James Pillans at the Red Lion Hotel, Bristol.  
The clerk raised an eyebrow.  
James was a little embarrassed.  
But he did it anyway.

**Scene 8 — Mother Sees the Advert (Shot 8.1)**  
Days later, in Sunderland, Norah’s mother opened the newspaper.  
She read the advert.  
And she smiled.

**Scene 9 — Mother Telephones Norah (Shot 9.1)**  
She telephoned Norah at her cousin’s and read it aloud.  
Norah laughed and said she wasn’t interested.  
After the call, her mother quietly wrote to James and invited him to tea.

**Scene 10 — Tea and Cake in the Garden (Shots 10.1–10.2)**  
When Norah came home, James knocked at the door.  
Her mother let him in.  
They found Norah in the garden.  
They sat with tea and cake and simply carried on the conversation they had begun on the train.  
As if no time had passed at all.

**Closing (over the fade)**  
James and Norah were married later that year.  
They had a daughter, Felicity.  
Felicity had three children.  
Those three had eight grandchildren.  
And those eight, so far, have five great-great-grandchildren.  
One of them is telling you this story.

---

## Timing notes
- The script is written to sit under the existing shot list without forcing cuts.  
- Longer lines sit under the longer shots (especially 4.2 and 10.2).  
- You can add short pauses where the picture needs to breathe (James stepping off, the palm-to-face moment, the smile over the newspaper).  
- Total spoken length is roughly 9–11 minutes at a gentle, storytelling pace.
