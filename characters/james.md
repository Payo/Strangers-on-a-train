# James Pillans

**Age in 1924:** 24  
**Background:** Middle-class family, south of England. Clever; later designed a cylindrical slide rule.

## Personality
Gentle soul, modest, good-looking. Shy quietness likely coloured by minor PTSD from the Western Front. Brainy and attractive — a powerful combination tempered by modesty and war experience.

## War
Lied about his age and joined the Royal Engineers in 1915. Posted to France. Saw terrible things; a number of his company were killed. Has had enough adventure.

## Want
A beautiful suburban life — an attractive, lively wife and a child. On the train itself he is simply travelling; the meeting is unplanned. Once talking he wants the conversation to continue and, by the end, her name and address.

## Need
After the war, to feel that ordinary life — connection, gentleness, a future — is still possible. Meeting Norah and finding the courage to place the advert begins to answer that need.

## Flaw / Limitation
Overwhelmed by Norah’s beauty. When he realises who he is sitting opposite, the rush of adrenaline triggers a mammalian flight response and he gets off the train. He later fails to ask for her name or address.

## Arc (Vonnegut fortune shape)
- Good fortune: sits opposite Norah  
- Ill-fortune: has to leave the carriage  
- Good fortune: returns at the next stop and sits opposite her (overcomes the flight response)  
- Ill-fortune: does not get her name or address  
- Good fortune: brave enough to place the advert  
- Good fortune: her mother sees it  
- Good fortune: they have tea  
- Good fortune: they fall in love  

Wobbly at the start, then a series of progressive steps toward happiness and love.

## Relationships
- **Norah:** Gentle, attentive, and increasingly smitten. Flight response is brief; he returns. Falls in love at first sight.
- **Own family:** Largely off-stage. Middle-class southern background and intellect sit quietly behind his modesty.
- **Practical tools:** The Sunderland newspaper and The Red Lion Hotel, Bristol.
