# Norah

**Age in 1924:** 28  
**Occupation:** French teacher  

## Personality
Quietly confident, curious, and a little independent. Fashionable and stylish (the bob haircut is part of her presentation). She receives attention from men and is mildly cautious with it. Warm and engaged in conversation once she chooses to join it.

## Background
Lived in France for a year as a teenager, just before the First World War. Returned to Sunderland feeling more sophisticated and interested in the wider world. Her father was a ship’s captain, which contributed to her sense of adventure. Ambitions clearly larger than Sunderland and the North-East. The post-war shortage of young men left a quiet worry about remaining a spinster, though this is lightly felt.

## Want
A peaceful summer holiday staying with her cousin near Bristol. A break from teaching. Not looking for a husband.

## Need
To feel that a life larger than Sunderland is still possible for her — that her sophistication, curiosity, and ambitions are not going to end in quiet spinsterhood.

## Flaw / Limitation
Self-contained to the point of mild caution. Enjoys the conversation with James but does not offer her name or address when they part.

## Arc
Begins self-contained and mildly cautious about romantic attention. By the end she has chosen to respond to James’s advert and invite him to visit — a small but decisive act of openness that matches her larger ambitions and leads to the lifelong romance.

## Relationships
- **Mother:** Affectionate. It is the mother who sees the newspaper advert while Norah is still on holiday. There is a moment of tension around the mother’s response; Norah is shocked by the suggestion to invite James, but secretly very happy.
- **Cousin:** Relaxed and at home.
- **James:** Warm and engaged during the nine-hour train conversation. Mildly cautious at the parting (gets off without looking back). Later chooses to invite him to tea.

## Notes
James is the one who falls in love at first sight. Norah treats the meeting as an amusing distraction until the advert arrives.
